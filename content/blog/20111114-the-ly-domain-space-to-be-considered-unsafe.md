It's beginning to [slowly dawn on people][1] that the process of snatching up domain names from obscure (and not so obscure) countries around the world may be about to backfire, badly. Libya has begun to issue various edicts regarding who can own their top-level domain, how they should be used and the type of content that can be viewed on them. While the country has a turbulent history, there are complaints being levelled around the web that appear to me to be slightly unjust, and based on the fact that Libyan law is difficult to understand or adhere to, or just doesn't marry up with Western values.

> &#8230;clause 3.5 clearly states that: "The Applicant certifies that, to the best of his/her knowledge the domain name is not being registered for any activities/purpose not permitted under Libyan law." Pornography and adult material aren't allowed under Libyan Law, therefore we removed the domain&#8230;

While I can appreciate that [Ben][2], and [many other companies][3], will be concerned that their domains may be lost and in some cases their business model may be irrevocably damaged due to this sudden enforcement of terms, I do wonder how a complaint can be levelled when these terms have existed, in English, for at least the last four or five years, (I know this as the previous owner of nt.ly). I also wonder how fair it is for people to level criticism at a country that is simply trying to maintain it's own moral code on content that is representing it, even if the link is rather tenuously.
